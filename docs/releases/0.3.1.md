# Teachouse 0.3.1 for Windows and Android

This is the beta that follows 0.3.0.
It is still a beta, so expect rough edges, and please say what you find.
The headline is the work you do to one resource: finding it, starting it before it is finished, and putting its files on it.
The Android app is fixed too — on a fresh install it closed itself the moment it opened, and it now reaches the sign-in page.
The console reaches browsers and existing installs the moment it is published, and 0.3.1 is the app build that carries the same console to Windows and Android.

## What you can do with it

Press Ctrl-K anywhere in the console — Cmd-K on a Mac — and a search box opens over the page you are on.
Type part of a title and it lists the resources that match, each with its price and the marketplaces it is on, and Enter opens the one you have highlighted.
It shows eight at a time, and where more match it says so and offers the full board rather than quietly ending the list.
A catalogue it could not read says exactly that, rather than looking like a catalogue with nothing in it.

A resource can now live on Teachouse without being on a marketplace and without a file.
That is the ordinary shape of something you are still working on: a title, a price and your own notes, kept here until it is ready.
A file becomes required the moment the resource goes on a marketplace, because a listing someone can pay for needs something for them to download.
So the create form asks for a marketplace and a file together, or for neither, and it says which of the two it is waiting on rather than refusing after you press the button.

The resource page now has a Files panel.
You can add a file, replace one and remove one, and each keeps the name you gave it rather than the name it happened to be stored under.
We draw the thumbnail ourselves from the first file, so replacing or removing that file redraws it from the one that takes its place — and the panel says so before you commit, not after you notice.
A resource that is on a marketplace keeps at least one file, so removing its last one is refused, and the refusal says to replace it or add another first.
A resource kept here alone can let its last file go, and its thumbnail goes with it rather than being redrawn.
The create form has thumbnail slots you can upload into as well.

Creating a listing on Tes now carries the licence, which is what was missing.
A licence has legal effect, so it stays your decision and nothing picks one for you: where the marketplace you chose requires one, the form shows the licence field and offers the values that marketplace accepts for a free or a paid resource.
Tes needs a rights declaration to go with your answer, and the app now composes that itself rather than sending a listing the marketplace would turn down.
A resource's title is also checked the same way whether you are creating it or editing it, in the same words both times.

The console reads more plainly than it did.
Wording has been rewritten across every page in ordinary words, so you read thumbnail rather than cover and a sentence rather than a term of art.
Every button, field and menu is now drawn to one height, so a row of controls lines up instead of stepping up and down; dialogs sit in the middle of the screen, and on a phone they open as a sheet across the full width.
The Marketplaces grid carries each marketplace's own mark — a square icon where one exists, and the name in its own lettering where it does not — so a tile is recognisable at a glance.

A page that cannot be drawn now says so instead of showing you nothing.
Where something goes wrong while the console is drawing a page, that page is replaced by a line saying it could not be drawn and a button that reloads it, and the rail and the page list around it keep working.
We are told each time it happens, which we were not before.

You now choose a name for your organisation.
A new seller meets that screen on first sign-in, before the console; if you were already working here, you meet a banner you can dismiss and choose later from Settings.
The name is between 3 and 32 characters of lowercase letters and digits, with single hyphens between words — not at the start or the end, and never two in a row — and a name this product already means something by, such as admin, help or settings, is refused with the reason said out loud.
It is yours alone, it appears beside the console's title, and you can change it afterwards.

Import now takes a spreadsheet as well as a shop.
Ask for the template and you get a workbook built from what each marketplace actually asks for, with a tab per destination and one for resources you are keeping here, the column names across the top, a marker under each showing what is required, and one worked example filled in for you.
Fill it in, upload it, and you get a report row by row, each refusal naming the spreadsheet row number you can see in Excel.
One upload carries up to 500 rows and 8 MB, and a batch you start and never finish is cleared after 14 days.
One rule is worth knowing before you start: every row on a marketplace tab has to name a file, whether it is going up as a draft or as a live listing, because a resource on a marketplace needs something a buyer can download; a row on the Teachouse tab needs none.

The import drain readout is gone from your pages.
It measured how much of your own vocabulary we still had questions about, which is our engineering problem rather than something you can act on, so it now sits on the operator pages and your Reconciliation page is the shorter for it.

## What changed in the app itself

The Android app no longer closes on first launch.
On a device that had never signed in, it drew its window and the whole app died about a second later, before it had shown you anything.
The cause was in the web view the app is built on, in the part that reads the browser's stored sign-ins: asked about a site it had no sign-in for, it stopped the app rather than answering "none".
We are carrying the fix ourselves until it is released upstream, and it is written up for the project that maintains that web view.
On Android the app now trusts a list of certificate authorities carried inside it rather than the one on the device, which is what let it verify Teachouse at all on that platform; the practical difference is that a certificate only your own device trusts, such as a school proxy's, is refused there and accepted on Windows.
The app also only offers to update itself on Windows now, because updating itself is not something it can do on Android.

Nothing else in the app changed.
Your marketplace sign-ins, the schedule and every marketplace request work exactly as they did in 0.3.0, and they still run on your own computer.

## What is not ready yet

The spreadsheet import stops at the report.
You can take the template, fill it, upload it and read what we made of every row, and the rows are held for you — but attaching the files to them and publishing the batch are the half that comes next, and neither is in this release.

The Android app has been proven only as far as the sign-in page, and only on an emulator.
It opens, it stays open and it reaches Teachouse; signing in from it, listing from it, and its own regular check with us have not been exercised on a device at all.
That test is the next thing we do, on a phone rather than an emulator, and what we find will decide whether anything else about Android has to change.

One seller reported the Resources page coming up empty.
We could not make it happen again, on any account or any catalogue we tried, so nothing here claims it is fixed.
What we did add is a check on the two ways that page could plausibly go blank — a resource that is on no marketplace at all must still be drawn as a row, and the board must re-read itself after you create something — and the console now fails its own tests if either stops holding.
If it turns out to be a third way we have not thought of, the page now says it could not be drawn rather than leaving you looking at nothing.
If you see it, please say so, and say what you had just done.

Everything listed as not ready in 0.3.0 is still not ready: Marketplace Sharing, both browser extensions, an Apple build, every marketplace beyond Tes and TPT, buying a subscription, and the privacy and terms pages.

## Updates

If you are on 0.3.0, this arrives on its own.
The app checks the beta channel each time it opens and installs a new version before it starts working.

If you are on 0.1.3 or earlier, install this version by hand, once.
Those builds cannot fetch it themselves.

The Android app does not update itself.
Download the new package and install it over the one you have.

The console needs no update at all.
It is served from Teachouse, so it is already what you see in a browser and what the app shows you when it opens, with nothing to install for it.

## Installing it

On Windows, download the `-setup.exe` installer and run it.
The `.msi` beside it installs the same application and exists for anyone who has to deploy it through Windows Installer.

Windows will warn you that the publisher is unknown.
That is expected for now: this build is not yet signed with a code-signing certificate, and the warning will go away in a later release.

On Android, download `Teachouse_0.3.1_arm64.apk`, which is built for 64-bit ARM devices.

The `SHA256SUMS.txt` file lists the checksum of each Windows installer, and `SHA256SUMS-android.txt` the checksum of the Android package, if you want to verify the download.
You can also get both builds from the console: the Marketplaces page has a Downloads section carrying each one with its version and its SHA-256 beside the button.

## For operators

The import-drain readout moves off the seller's Reconciliation page and onto a new operator page, grouped by organisation, with the count of rows it could not read stated beside the series rather than dropped silently.

Five migrations land with this release: 0057 the organisation name, 0058 the import batch and its rows, 0059 the seller-chosen file name, 0060 the operator's drain read, and 0061 moving the file requirement off the resource and onto its marketplace listing.

Migration 0060 gives `tam_backoffice` the read it needs and no more.
It is the ninth entry in migration 0037's enumerated list and the only one whose policy is not `USING (true)`: it opens exactly the rows recording a drain measurement and leaves every other job, item, settlement and failure event a tenant has written unreadable to that role.
The restriction sits in the policy rather than in the view because `job_event` forces row-level security, so a view running as its owner would return zero rows against an unpinned tenant — an empty page reporting nothing wrong, which is the dangerous failure rather than the loud one.

`backoffice_grants.rs` is the closed-world test over that boundary.
It probes the operator role rather than trusting the grant list: what it may read across tenants, that it may write none of it, and that the credential vault stays out of its reach exactly as it stays out of the application's.
A grant added to that role without a line in this test is a failure, not an oversight caught in review.

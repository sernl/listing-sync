# Teachouse 0.20.0

## Copy

- **Refunds are stated plainly (terms section 9).** The clause "unless we agree or New Zealand law requires it" is gone. Move Packs: unused, within 14 days, full refund. Monthly plans: a month that has started is not refunded, and cancelling stops the next renewal. Yearly plans: a refund can be asked for at any time during the year. It covers the whole months not yet started, less one month's fee (yearly price × (unused whole months − 1) ÷ 12, never below zero), and the plan ends on the day of the refund. An Explain under the yearly rule works through an example on Pro's yearly price from the plan table: asked during month 4, 8 months are unused and the refund is 7 × $240 ÷ 12 = $140. The section's "In short" line, the terms' "Can I get a refund?" answer and the pricing FAQ say the same, and the FAQ links to `/terms/#refunds`. Section 15 (the Consumer Guarantees Act) is unchanged.
- **No more "young and still changing".** Section 11 now opens "we keep Teachouse running and improve it over time", and the line about features being "marked as early or coming soon" is gone. Nothing else on the landing described Teachouse as young, new, small or a startup.
- **Words no longer run together.** Astro drops a line break between text and a tag or an expression, so the terms read "contact@teachouse.iofrom", "Privacy Policysays", and the comparison table read "AI description fillComing soon". All three are fixed. The new `just landing-spacing-gate` (`apps/landing/scripts/check-spacing.mjs`) reads the built pages for a word touching an `a`, `strong`, `em`, `code` or `span` tag, and the `.astro` sources for text meeting a `{expression}` across a line break. It runs in `just landing-check`, after the copy gate.
- The pricing page's Explain style is now the shared `.explain` class (was `.move-explain`), which the terms use too.

Deploy: the terms changed in substance, so their version moves to `2026-10-04` (`TERMS_VERSION`, which the signup-consent change generates and which drives the terms page's "Last updated" and "Effective" dates). Section 18 promises 30 days' email notice before a change that matters; decide whether to send that notice or treat the refund change as in readers' favour.

## Sign-up consent

Nobody gets an account without agreeing to the terms first, and the agreement is kept as a record.

- **Two boxes on the sign-up form.** Above "Create account": "I agree to the Terms of Service and the Privacy Policy, and I confirm that I own or hold the rights to every resource I publish through Teachouse." (linking `teachouse.io/terms/` and `/privacy/`) and "I am 18 or older." Create account and the Google/Microsoft buttons stay disabled until both are ticked. The old "By signing up you agree" line is gone.
- **The identity service refuses a sign-up without them.** `/sign-up/email` answers 422 "Please tick both boxes to continue." unless the body carries `consent: { terms_privacy, ip_ownership, age_18: true, version }`. A social sign-up sends the same object as `additionalData.consent` with `requestSignUp: true`. The before hook validates it and puts it in better-auth's signed OAuth state (`addOAuthServerContext`), so the choice survives the provider redirect and the record is written at the callback. This replaces the sessionStorage hand-off the brief suggested, because that would have let an account exist between the callback and a later client write. Google and Microsoft now have `disableImplicitSignUp`, so signing in with a provider from the sign-in page for an account that does not exist yet comes back with `?error=signup_disabled` instead of creating an account with no agreement. The sign-in page then says "No Teachouse account uses that sign-in yet. Create one on the sign-up page first."
- **Recorded the moment the account exists, or not at all.** Once the account is created, tam-auth posts the three statements to tam-server's new `POST /internal/consent` (shared secret in `x-tam-internal-secret`, the same secret as `/internal/address`). tam-server writes them to the app database, so the operator pool can read them. If the write fails, or no consent URL or secret is configured, tam-auth deletes the account it just made, clears the session cookie, and answers 503 "We could not record your agreement, so your account was not created. Please try again in a minute." A social sign-up goes back to `/signup?error=consent_unavailable` with that sentence.
- **Migration 0103, `account_consent`.** One row per statement (`terms_privacy`, `ip_ownership`, `age_18`): identity subject, email, `document_version` (the Terms' effective date), `accepted_at` (server time), `ip_address inet`, `user_agent`, `created_at`. The table is global, with no row-level security and no foreign key to the identity schema, so the record outlives the account. It is append-only: UPDATE, DELETE and TRUNCATE are revoked from `tam_app`, and statement triggers refuse UPDATE and DELETE even for the owner. `tam_backoffice` has SELECT.
- **One version, two readers.** `TERMS_VERSION` (`2026-10-04`) and `PRIVACY_VERSION` (`2026-10-02`) are defined once, in `crates/tam-api/src/account_consent.rs`. tam-typegen emits them to `web/src/lib/generated/legal.ts` (re-exported by `web/src/lib/legal.ts`) and `apps/landing/src/legal.generated.js`. The landing's Terms page prints its Last updated and Effective dates from that value, and the Privacy page prints its Last updated date the same way. `just web-check` diffs both files.
- **The terms change, everyone agrees again.** `GET /v1/consent/status` says whether the signed-in person has agreed to the current `TERMS_VERSION`. If they have not, the console shows a sheet they cannot dismiss, with the same two boxes and "I agree" (`POST /v1/consent`, which records their IP and browser too, and answers 409 to a page left open across a change). Accounts made before this release have no record, so they see the sheet once on their next visit. Users with no identity subject (a minted dev login) are never asked. The sheet is not shown while an operator is impersonating.
- **Operators can see it.** The Users page has a Consent column ("✓ when · version", or "none" for an account that predates the boxes), from `GET /v1/admin/consents`. The user detail sheet lists every statement with time, IP and browser, from `GET /v1/admin/users/{subject}/consent`.

### Deploy

- Migration 0103 runs on start.
- tam-server: new `--consent-secret-file <path>`, a file holding the same value as tam-auth's `TAM_AUTH_INTERNAL_SECRET`. In the Nix module, `services.teachouse.server.consentSecretFile` defaults to `server.mail.authInternalSecretFile`, which already holds that value. Without it the route answers 404, and tam-auth then refuses every sign-up (startup logs say so on both sides, and the module warns).
- tam-auth: new `TAM_AUTH_CONSENT_URL`. The module sets it to `http://127.0.0.1:<server.port>/internal/consent`. `TAM_AUTH_INTERNAL_SECRET` must be set (`auth.internalSecretFile`). Locally, `just auth-env` now generates the secret for a new `auth/.env`, and `just dev-all` hands it to tam-server. An existing `auth/.env` needs `TAM_AUTH_CONSENT_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8080/internal/consent` and a `TAM_AUTH_INTERNAL_SECRET` added by hand.
- `/internal/consent` is served on the public origin like every other tam-server path, fenced by the secret alone. Blocking `/internal/*` at the edge (Caddy) is a cheap second fence.

## AccountDelete

Sellers can delete their own account from Account settings.

- **Console.** A "Delete your account" panel sits at the foot of Account settings (`/settings`), in the warning palette. It says what happens in one sentence: the account, the catalogue and the device registrations go; listings on the marketplaces stay; it cannot be undone. Explain holds the detail: a plan is cancelled at Stripe straight away with no refund beyond the Terms, packs of moves are forfeited, and the files on the seller's devices are untouched. The button opens a sheet that asks for the account's name (or `DELETE`), the current password where the account has one, and an optional reason. An account with no password (Google, Microsoft, passkey only) must have signed in within the last few minutes; otherwise the sheet signs the seller out and sends them to sign in again, and `/settings?delete=1` brings them back with the sheet open. On success the console signs out and lands on `https://teachouse.io/?deleted=1`, where the home page shows a one-line goodbye.
- **API.** `DELETE /v1/account` (session) with `{confirm, password?, reason?}`. In order: an operator is refused with 422 (`refusal: operator`; step down first), an organisation with another member with 409 (`shared_organisation`), a confirmation that is neither the slug nor `DELETE` with 422 (`confirmation`). Then tam-auth checks the proof (403 `password` / `reauthenticate`, 422 `password_required`). Then a live Stripe subscription is cancelled immediately (`DELETE /v1/subscriptions/{id}`, no proration or refund) and its status recorded. Then the goodbye mail "Your Teachouse account is deleted" goes out (`notify::compose_goodbye`), while the address is still known. Then tam-auth deletes the identity. Last, the platform user and organisation are erased through the same path the operator's deletion uses (`account::erase` → `ErasureRepo`), and an `account_deletion` row is written in the same transaction. The answer is 200 `{subscription_cancelled, goodbye_sent}` with the session cookie expired. A deployment without the mail set answers 503 and deletes nothing.
- **Organisations are one person each.** Signing in provisions an organisation per identity and nothing adds a member, so deleting the account erases the organisation. `app_user.org_id` is not unique, so the schema allows a second member. An organisation that has one is refused (409), as the operator's deletion already refuses it, rather than erased.
- **tam-auth.** Two internal routes sit beside `/internal/address/{subject}`, behind the same `TAM_AUTH_INTERNAL_SECRET` header, and answer `POST` only. `/internal/reauth/{subject}` takes `{password, sessionCookie}` and answers `{email, name}`, or a `refusal`: the password for an account with a `credential` row, otherwise a session in the forwarded cookie that is this subject's own and younger than five minutes (`auth/src/offboarding.ts`). tam-api forwards only the `better-auth.session_token` cookie. `/internal/delete/{subject}` deletes the user row. Sessions, linked accounts and passkeys cascade with it, and the act is recorded as `user_removed` with the user as both parties.
- **Migration 0104** adds `account_deletion` (subject, SHA-256 of the lower-cased address, org_id, requested_at, stripe_subscription_id, reason). It is global, with no row-level security, and `tam_backoffice` has SELECT on it. The domain database still holds no seller address.

Deploy: run the app migrations (0104). There is no tam-auth migration. Self-service deletion needs the mail set that is already configured in production (`--resend-api-key-file`, `--auth-internal-url`, `--auth-internal-secret-file` and the rest), because it reaches tam-auth through the same internal secret.

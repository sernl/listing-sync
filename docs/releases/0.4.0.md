# Teachouse 0.4.0 for Windows and Android

This is the beta that follows 0.3.4, and it is the largest change Teachouse has had.
It is still a beta, so expect rough edges, and please say what you find.
Three things stand out: Teachouse has a new look, with a dark theme; Tes is one marketplace rather than three regions; and importing your shop is now a job you watch, with Teachouse finding the resources you already have before it creates anything.
Under those, every organisation now holds a plan, and the console says plainly what each plan includes.
Almost all of this reaches you from the server, so a browser has it the moment it is published, and 0.4.0 is the app build that carries the same console, the new icon and the new import to Windows and Android.

## What changed in the app

The app opens on your console, not on the front page.
Its window now goes straight to the console's home, so the marketing page no longer flashes past on the way in, and on a phone there is no page behind the console for the back gesture to find.

The app follows your theme.
Choose System, Light or Dark under Account and the window's own chrome follows the page, on Windows and on Android alike.

The app reads your shop for an import.
When you start an import from Tes or TPT in the console, this device lists what your shop holds, then, once you have ticked what to bring, opens each chosen resource on your own computer or phone and describes it to Teachouse.
The file never leaves your device: what is sent is a fingerprint of it, its size, the page count for a PDF and a small picture of the cover, which is enough for Teachouse to tell whether you already have it.
Progress appears on the import's own page as each one is read.

Each cycle of work tells you once.
When the app finishes a run of work it raises one notification on this device saying what it did, on Windows in the notification area and on Android in the shade, one per run and never one per resource.
On Android the app asks once for permission to notify.

A new icon.
The app, its shortcut and its installer carry the Teachouse house mark in the new colours.

## What changed in the console

Teachouse has a new look.
Indigo, Teal, Lavender and Peach on Cream, Poppins headings over Inter body text, pill buttons, and the TeacHouse mark whose H is a house.
Under Account, Preferences gains a theme switch, System, Light or Dark, remembered by the browser you set it in; the dark theme re-grounds the same colours on Indigo, and every pair of text and background has been measured on both grounds.
The rail reads Import, Crosslist, Automations, Marketplaces and Account.

Tes is one marketplace.
Where the console used to show Tes GB, Tes US and Tes NZ, it now shows Tes.
Prices on Tes are in pounds, ages are age ranges, the subject tree is Tes's own, and the country your Tes account sells from is a field on the Tes connection, set to the United Kingdom unless you change it.
The status page shows one row per marketplace, and the strip at the top of every page no longer says which sites it is checking.

The new-resource form is written for teachers.
Marketplaces come first, as tiles with each marketplace's mark, one tick each.
Files drop onto the form or come in by Browse, several at once.
A Preview box lets you pick the pages that make the preview and lays your name across each one as a watermark, made on your own computer before anything is uploaded.
The description has a formatting bar; grades read as Years or as American grades on one toggle; the panels that only one marketplace needs say so in their heading; and every error is a button that takes you to the field.

Importing your shop is a job you watch.
Import lists one card per source: a spreadsheet in our template, read on our server, and each marketplace you have connected, read on your own device.
A marketplace import starts as a run: your device lists the shop, you tick what to bring, and only what you tick is opened.
A bar on the run's page counts what has been read, what needs you and what has arrived, as it happens.
Past runs are listed under Import, each opening its own page.

Teachouse finds what you already have before it creates anything.
As each resource is described, it is compared with your catalogue.
A file that is byte for byte one you already hold is joined to the resource you have, and the run says so.
Two that look alike without being identical are put to you as a pair, with one sentence saying why, such as the text of both PDFs being 92% the same, and three answers: Same resource, keep one; Different resources; Decide later.
Nothing is merged or created until you answer, and an answer of Different is remembered so you are never asked about that pair again.
Where Same joins two resources you already had, Teachouse keeps the record needed to undo it for thirty days; the button for that is not yet in the console, so for now ask us and we will reverse it.
A spreadsheet import runs the same comparison before it commits.

Every imported resource carries its marketplace as a label.
The labels Tes and TPT are Teachouse's own: they are applied by the import, keep their colour, do not count against your labels, and are not removed when you edit a resource's other labels.

TPT can be read.
Starting an import from TPT lists your shop and brings the title, price and grades of each resource.
Because Teachouse cannot yet download a TPT resource's file, a TPT import is never joined to a resource you already have on its own; it is put to you as a pair instead.

Every organisation holds a plan.
The Subscription page under Account shows your plan, what it includes, how much of each allowance you have used, and the two ways to buy: the subscription, and a one-off Catalogue Import at one of five rungs, twenty, fifty, one hundred, two hundred and fifty or five hundred resources, with a conversation above that.
Founding 100 members see their offer and its end date.
Every control the plan does not include is shown disabled with its reason in a sentence, rather than hidden.
An account that bought a one-off import sees Import, Migrations, Export and Account until it subscribes.

## What changed on the site

The front page is rebuilt to the new brand.
It draws the Resource Atelier: resource covers, a catalogue card, routing lines to the marketplace marks, and a real screenshot of the console.
It reads in the dark theme when your browser asks for it.

Pricing on the front page comes from the same table the console reads.
The Catalogue Import ladder runs to five hundred, import is included in the subscription, the Founding 100 discount runs for three years, and AI fill is announced as coming soon, at two hundred fills a month.

## Known limitations

An import from a marketplace needs that marketplace connected on this device.
Starting one without a connection is refused with a sentence saying to connect the marketplace first.

Importing has been exercised against a Tes shop through the app's own tests and against a live console with described resources, not yet against a seller's real Tes shop on a handset.
The comparison that finds resources you already have is tuned on the fixtures in the tests; the thresholds may need moving once real shops have been through it, and the pairs it puts to you are the place to tell us.

TPT resources cannot yet be joined automatically to ones you hold, because their files cannot be downloaded; every TPT match is a pair for you to answer.

Migrations, scheduling, templates, collections and the backoffice are laid out in the plan of record and not yet built; Marketplace Sharing is still inert.
AI fill is announced and not built.

A subscription and a one-off import can be bought only where the operator has connected the price list; on the live service the buttons are present and the checkout is dormant until then.

Email about a finished run is not yet being sent; the Notifications page and the device's own notification are where a finished run is announced.

Pressing back on Android with a picker or the search sheet open still moves you a screen rather than closing the sheet.
The tab bar at the foot still sits under the gesture pill on a phone that navigates by gestures.

There is no browser extension for Chrome or Firefox, and no Apple build.
No marketplace beyond Tes and TPT is connected.
The privacy and terms pages are still drafts.

## Updating

If you are on 0.3.4 on Windows, this arrives on its own.
The app checks the beta channel each time it opens and installs a new version before it starts working.

The Android app does not update itself.
Download the package from this release and install it over the one you have.

The console needs no update at all.
It is served from Teachouse, so it is already what you see in a browser and what the app shows you when it opens, with nothing to install for it.

## Installing it

Windows will warn you that the publisher is unknown.
That is expected for now: this build is not yet signed with a code-signing certificate, and the warning will go away in a later release.

## For operators

Six migrations land with this release, 0064, 0065 and 0067 through 0070; 0066 was never issued.

Migration 0068 makes Tes one marketplace.
It refuses to run where any organisation holds one product on two Tes inventories, naming the organisation, so that case is reconciled by hand before the migration is retried; it rewrites tenant rows in place, drops unbound Curriculum ticks, reseeds the derived projection tables and adds `connection.country`, defaulted to `GB`.
It lifts forced row-level security on the tables it reads for the length of one transaction and restores it before committing.

Migration 0069 creates `entitlement_grant`; every organisation's plan is the strongest unexpired grant and free with none.
An operator grants or revokes a plan on any organisation through the backoffice with a reason and an audit row.
`tam-server` takes `--paddle-price-map <path>`, a JSON object from Paddle price id to plan and rung; without it the checkout is dormant and the webhook grants nothing.
The NixOS module carries it as `services.teachouse.server.paddlePriceMap`.

Migration 0070 creates `import_run`, `import_run_item`, `product_fingerprint`, `duplicate_verdict` and `duplicate_evidence`, and adds `label.system`.
Routes land under `/{version}/imports/runs` and `/{version}/duplicates`, and the ledger gains four event kinds for a run's progress.
A run for a marketplace with no linked connection is refused at creation.

The desktop client's window navigates to `/app` at start-up; the landing page's redirect script stays for clients older than this one.

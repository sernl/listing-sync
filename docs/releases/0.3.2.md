# Teachouse 0.3.2 for Windows and Android

This is the beta that follows 0.3.1.
It is still a beta, so expect rough edges, and please say what you find.
The headline is the round of things you told us were wrong: the app did not know which machine it was running on, a phone showed you the website squeezed rather than an app, the Resources page refused to draw at all, and there was no way in from a screen to connect a marketplace or to disconnect one.
Almost all of this reaches you from the server, so a browser has it the moment it is published, and 0.3.2 is the app build that carries the same console to Windows and Android.

## What changed in the app

Every computer and phone running the app now puts itself on your list.
The app registers the machine it is on as soon as the console finishes loading there, so a machine that never appeared under Your machines appears without you doing anything.
A machine whose registration was lost registers itself again on its next check-in rather than going quiet.

Your phone is now named after itself.
On Android the app had no name it could offer, so a phone that did register would sit in the list under a name no one would recognise.
It now uses the maker and model the phone reports about itself, and that is what you see under Your machines: the model exactly as the phone gives it, and the maker's name with its first letter raised where the phone writes it in lower case.
A Galaxy Note10+ appears as Samsung SM-N975F.

A link to a marketplace now opens in your own browser.
Before this, following a listing link did something different and wrong on each platform: nothing at all on one, a bare window inside the app on another, and on the phone the marketplace replacing Teachouse itself.

Connecting a marketplace is now something you can do from a screen.
In the app on a computer, each marketplace card has its own Connect button, which opens that marketplace's own login page for you to sign in to.
In a browser, and on the phone, the card says where it can be done instead: connect from the Teachouse app on your computer.

## What changed in the console

Your catalogue is called Resources everywhere now.
The board that was at Inventory is at Resources, which is the name it already had in the menu, and every old link and bookmark redirects to the new one, carrying any search you had saved in it.

The Resources page draws again.
Some accounts met a sentence saying the page could not be drawn instead of the board, and the same account could meet it on Marketplaces too.
The cause was two parts of the console disagreeing about the shape of one answer from the server, so which page you opened first decided whether the next one worked.
Both now read that answer through one reader, and a page that does fail to draw no longer keeps failing on every page you open after it.

On a phone the console is laid out for a phone.
The top bar is now the account avatar on its own, with the account under it, and search has moved down to the bar at the bottom of the screen where the sections and the new-resource button already were.
The marketplace chips on each resource used to run off the side of the card; they now stay inside it, capped at two with a marker showing how many are hidden, which opens the rest when you tap it.

You can sign out on a phone.
Log out now sits at the top of the Preferences page, under Account and beside Subscription, which is the only place a phone can reach it: the menu card that carries the other one is hidden at that width.

Notifications can be closed.
The messages across the top of a page, and the small ones that appear in the corner, now carry a close button large enough to hit on a phone.
Escape closes the one you have moved to as well, and puts you back where you were.
A message that explains something about the product stays closed on that browser once you close it, while a message about something that just happened comes back the next time it happens.
An error message no longer disappears on its own, because a sentence saying what did not happen is the one you most need to keep.

You can disconnect a marketplace.
A connected card now offers Disconnect, and it says what that does before you agree: scheduled work for that marketplace stops, nothing is removed from the marketplace itself, and your listings stay here as records.
It is reversible, and connecting again is the same button.
Doing it in the app removes that marketplace's login from the machine first.
Doing it in a browser cannot, because the login is on whichever machine holds it and never on our servers, so the page says so and tells you to disconnect in the app on that machine or to sign that machine out under Your machines.

Automations no longer counts a marketplace you have disconnected.
Its sync card reads a marketplace you disconnected as gone rather than as one you still have, so it says no marketplace is connected instead of showing the work as ready to run.

Editing a resource now uses the same form as creating one.
Every field the create form has, the edit form has, including the thumbnails, the subjects and year levels, and the licence wording.
A marketplace can be added while editing but not taken away, and one already ticked says why it cannot be unticked.

An edit is held to the same marketplace rules as a create.
Changing a price, or another field, on a resource that is on a marketplace is now checked against what that marketplace requires, even where the edit sends none of that marketplace's own details.
Where something required is missing, the edit is refused in the same words the create shows you, and nothing is saved.

You can take back a file you uploaded by mistake.
The upload on the create form now has a Remove control beside the file it accepted, so a wrong file can be dropped rather than only covered over by a second one.
Removing it does not reclaim storage: the file itself stays in your storage either way, and the form says so beside the control.

A cover picture that cannot be shown leaves the empty tile in its place.
Where the picture will not draw in your browser, the form falls back to the tile you see before there is one, rather than the torn-picture mark a browser draws for something it cannot display.

Every marketplace tile now says what that marketplace is.
All twenty-one carry a sentence or two written for a seller who does not recognise the name: where it is, who buys there, and whether it is a marketplace at all or a shop of your own that you bring buyers to.

The three download tiles carry a mark each, a computer for Windows, a phone for Android and a laptop for Apple, so they are told apart at a glance.

## What changed on the site

The public site no longer writes as though there were exactly two marketplaces.
It speaks of the marketplaces you sell on, with one sentence that still says plainly which connections work today.

The front page now shows the marketplaces themselves.
Under the opening lines there is a band of marketplace marks, each linking to that marketplace's own site, with a note under it saying which of them connect today, that those names and logos belong to their own companies, and that Teachouse is independent of all of them.

Teachouse has one set of colours.
A deep forest green carries the buttons, a jade green the highlights and a soft violet the wash behind the front page's opening, and the site and the console now draw from the same set rather than each having its own.
The marks come from it too: the icon in a browser tab, the one a phone keeps on its home screen, and the app's own icon on Windows and on Android.
So does the page the app shows you when it cannot reach Teachouse.

## Known limitations

Etsy, Shopify and Boom Learning appear in the front page's band by name rather than by their logo.
Each of the three requires written permission before its mark may be used, and we have not asked for it yet.

There is no dark theme yet.
The colours are now defined once in one place, which is what a dark theme needs, but only the light one is built.

The emails Teachouse sends are still in the older colours.
An email carries its own colours rather than reading the console's, so bringing them across is a separate decision and it has not been taken.

Connecting a marketplace still has to be done on a computer.
The marketplace sign-in itself needs the app on a computer, and on the phone the marketplace's card says so rather than offering you a Connect button.

Registering a phone as a machine has been exercised on an emulator and in the app's own tests, not yet on a phone signed in to a live account.
Your phone is the first live check, and Your machines is where it should appear.

Following a marketplace link out to your own browser has been exercised in the app's own tests, not yet on a phone.

Editing several resources at once is still not built.
Editing them one at a time works, and the board says so where the bulk control would be.

Much of what 0.3.1 said was not ready is still not ready.
The spreadsheet import stops at the report: asking for the template, filling it, uploading it and reading what we made of every row all work, and attaching the files and publishing the batch are the half still to come.
Marketplace Sharing is laid out and does nothing.
There is no browser extension for Chrome or Firefox, and no Apple build.
No marketplace beyond Tes and TPT is connected.
A subscription cannot be bought yet.
The privacy and terms pages are still drafts.

## Updating

If you are on 0.3.1 on Windows, this arrives on its own.
The app checks the beta channel each time it opens and installs a new version before it starts working.

The Android app does not update itself.
Download the package from this release and install it over the one you have.

The console needs no update at all.
It is served from Teachouse, so it is already what you see in a browser and what the app shows you when it opens, with nothing to install for it.

## Installing it

Windows will warn you that the publisher is unknown.
That is expected for now: this build is not yet signed with a code-signing certificate, and the warning will go away in a later release.

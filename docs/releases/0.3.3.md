# Teachouse 0.3.3 for Windows and Android

This is the beta that follows 0.3.2.
It is still a beta, so expect rough edges, and please say what you find.
The headline is the resource form, which is now one page instead of a stack of nine cards, and a round of smaller repairs, several of them to things a phone made awkward.
The spreadsheet import also reaches the point where it makes resources rather than only a report.
Almost all of this reaches you from the server, so a browser has it the moment it is published, and 0.3.3 is the app build that carries the same console to Windows and Android.

## What changed in the app

Disconnecting a marketplace now takes effect on that machine at once.
When you disconnect in the app, the machine tells us what it still holds before the page answers, so its row under Your machines stops listing that login in the same press.
Before this the row went on saying the marketplace was connected there — for up to an hour on a computer, and until you next opened the app on a phone — while the marketplace's own card said the opposite, and there was no way to tell which of the two was true.

There is a Check in now button beside Your machines.
It appears only inside the app, because only a machine can report for itself, and it makes the list current without waiting for the next scheduled check-in or closing and reopening the app.
When the machine cannot reach us it says so in its own words, so a list that did not change reads as a cause you can act on rather than as nothing having happened.

A phone no longer does a full round of work every time you bring it forward.
It still checks in each time you bring it forward, because that is the only way it learns it has been signed out, but it goes looking for marketplace work once an hour at most, which is the interval a computer already keeps.
Opening the app twenty times in an hour used to cost twenty full rounds of work from the handset.

Work your own computer runs now shows as finished when it finishes.
A sync or a migration the app ran there reports what it came to, and the item stops saying it is in progress the moment the run is over.
Before this the result never reached the page at all: the item stayed marked as in progress until a later sweep noticed it, and nothing said how the run had gone.

The top bar in the Android app now draws clear of the status bar.
It used to sit underneath the clock and the battery indicator.
This was watched on an Android 16 emulator rather than on a real phone, so it is worth telling us about if anything still sits under a bar.
The tab bar at the foot is a separate matter and is not fixed here: on a phone that navigates by gestures it still sits under the gesture pill.

## What changed in the console

Creating and editing a resource is one page rather than nine cards.
The destination tabs sit at the head of a single surface, each section below them is separated from the next by a hairline instead of by its own card and shadow, and every section opens with a small mark and a short heading.
The actions sit in a band at the foot: what the two choices mean on the left, then Cancel and either Create draft or Save changes on the right.
Nothing the form does has changed — the same fields, the same refusals under the same sections, and a marketplace already carrying the listing still cannot be unticked.

A required field now says the word Required.
It used to be an asterisk, which a screen reader reads out as punctuation; the word is read out as a word.
A field that says nothing beside its label is optional, and that absence is the only signal there is.

A worksheet put into a thumbnail slot is refused before it is stored.
The slot says that a thumbnail has to be a picture — a JPEG, a PNG or a GIF — and nothing is sealed and nothing is counted against your storage.
The check reads the file itself rather than its name, so renaming a worksheet to end in .jpg no longer gets it past, and the answer is the same whether the file was chosen through the picker or dropped on the slot.

A disconnect that only half succeeded now says which half.
Where the login has already been taken off the machine but the marketplace is still connected here, the message says exactly that, and that scheduled work has therefore not stopped, instead of saying that nothing happened.

An address the console does not know now lands on a Teachouse page.
Signed in, it says the page is not here and offers the way back to your resources; signed out, you are taken to the sign-in screen, rather than either of you meeting a bare white page with a number on it.

Closing one notification puts you back where you actually were.
Two messages on screen at once used to share one memory of what you had been doing, so closing the second returned you to whatever the first had interrupted; each now keeps its own.

Several things on a phone are now large enough to hit.
The help control in the header band, the items inside a kebab menu, and the marker standing for the marketplaces a card has no room to show are all raised to a thumb's size.
The marketplace tiles in the Resources filter are one size each, instead of four across the top with the fifth stretched to the full width underneath.

A spreadsheet import now reaches your catalogue.
Download the template from Import, fill one row for each resource, upload it, and read what we made of every row on a page of its own.
Where the report is clean you attach the files: drop them all at once, and each one goes to the row that named it, while anything naming no row, or more than one, waits below for you to place by hand.
The panel says how much storage you have left after each upload, and a file put on the wrong row can be taken off it again.
One button then shows what will happen — this many resources will be created, and the rows refused when the sheet was read will be left alone — and a confirmation creates them, a few at a time, with each row's outcome and a link to the resource it made.
Nothing is sent to a marketplace by any of it: the resources are in your catalogue here, and publishing them runs later, from your own device.

## Known limitations

Publishing an imported batch to a marketplace is still a separate step.
The import creates the resources and stops there; sending them on is the ordinary send, run from your own device, and the import does not start it for you.

Etsy, Shopify and Boom Learning appear in the front page's band by name rather than by their logo.
Each of the three requires written permission before its mark may be used, and we have not asked for it yet.

There is no dark theme yet.
The colours are defined once in one place, which is what a dark theme needs, but only the light one is built.

The emails Teachouse sends are still in the older colours.
An email carries its own colours rather than reading the console's, so bringing them across is a separate decision and it has not been taken.

Connecting a marketplace still has to be done on a computer.
The marketplace sign-in itself needs the app on a computer, and on the phone the marketplace's card says so rather than offering you a Connect button.

Registering a phone as a machine has still been exercised on an emulator and in the app's own tests, not yet on a phone signed in to a live account.
Your phone is still the first live check, and Your machines is where it should appear.

Following a marketplace link out to your own browser has been exercised in the app's own tests, not yet on a phone.

The hourly limit on how often a phone goes looking for marketplace work has been exercised in the app's own tests, not yet on a phone.

Editing several resources at once is still not built.
Editing them one at a time works, and the board says so where each bulk control would be.

Marketplace Sharing is laid out and does nothing.
There is no browser extension for Chrome or Firefox, and no Apple build.
No marketplace beyond Tes and TPT is connected.
A subscription cannot be bought yet.
The privacy and terms pages are still drafts.

## Updating

If you are on 0.3.2 on Windows, this arrives on its own.
The app checks the beta channel each time it opens and installs a new version before it starts working.

The Android app does not update itself.
Download the package from this release and install it over the one you have.

The console needs no update at all.
It is served from Teachouse, so it is already what you see in a browser and what the app shows you when it opens, with nothing to install for it.

## Installing it

Windows will warn you that the publisher is unknown.
That is expected for now: this build is not yet signed with a code-signing certificate, and the warning will go away in a later release.
